I submit the manuscript ``\textbf{Governance Reform Unlocks Decarbonization Dead Zones in Public Procurement}'' for consideration as an Article in \textit{Nature Sustainability}.

\textbf{Summary}

This manuscript identifies a climate-implementation bottleneck in public procurement: green criteria are least effective where governments lack credible supplier choice. Using 21.6 million government contracts from 27 countries, it shows that 22 ``Decarbonization Dead Zones'' place an estimated \texteuro190--250 billion of annual EU spending in markets where high carbon intensity coincides with persistent single-bidder awards.

This submission contributes three linked findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item In EU-context countries, competitive contracts are more common in higher-carbon sectors, yielding a $-4.3\%$ allocative portfolio premium ($t=-110$; $d=-0.08$).
    \item Directive 2014/24/EU is associated with a $-7.2$ percentage-point reduction in single-bidder rates in the preferred staggered Callaway \& Sant'Anna design (RMSPE permutation $p=0.042$); leave-one-out bounds remain $-4.75$ to $-8.46$ pp, and an external CanadaBuys procurement-method proxy-control gives $-3.6$ pp ($p=0.0068$).
    \item The allocative channel is corroborated across independent facility-, sector-, and supplier-level evidence, including a 43\% within-sector EU ETS emissions gap, a 66\% post-reform narrowing in Eurostat, annual E-PRTR facility matching, negative within-supplier comparisons, and a roughly fivefold increase in access to SBTi-validated suppliers when moving from one to five bidders in Dead Zone sectors.
\end{itemize}

We believe this contribution fits \textit{Nature Sustainability} because it identifies a missing institutional precondition for climate-effective procurement: before governments can use GPP to select cleaner suppliers, they must first open carbon-intensive markets to competition. The paper therefore presents governance reform not as administrative clean-up, but as an implementable climate-policy lever with immediate relevance at the scale of \texteuro190--250 billion in annual EU procurement spending.
